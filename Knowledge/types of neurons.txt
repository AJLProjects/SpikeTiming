Okay, let's break down these concepts.

1. Are regular excitatory neurons better at getting signals down range?

Yes, generally speaking, the primary role of most "regular" excitatory neurons (especially principal/projection neurons like pyramidal cells) is long-distance communication.
Specialization: They are structurally and functionally specialized for this. They typically have long axons that project out of the local area, sometimes traveling centimeters to connect different brain regions (e.g., cortex to thalamus, cortex to contralateral cortex via corpus callosum, cortex to brainstem/spinal cord).
Information Transmission: Their primary function is often to carry the results of local computations to other areas for further processing or to initiate actions.
Contrast with Interneurons: Most inhibitory interneurons, as discussed, have axons that ramify locally. Their primary role is to modulate the activity within their immediate vicinity, not typically to send signals far away (though there are exceptions, like some long-range projecting GABAergic neurons, but they are less common than long-range excitatory neurons).
So, while interneurons are crucial for shaping local activity, the excitatory projection neurons are the main communication lines for sending signals across significant distances in the brain.

2. Different Cell Types (Focusing on Excitatory/Inhibitory & Projection/Interneuron distinctions)

Here's a simplified overview of key neuronal types based on these distinctions:

A. Excitatory Neurons
    Title: The "Go" Signals / Long-Range Communicators
    Primary Neurotransmitter: Glutamate
    Primary Effect: Excitatory (increase the likelihood of postsynaptic firing)
    Typical Firing Pattern: Often "Regular Spiking" (RS) - show spike frequency adaptation.
    Common Examples & Roles:
    - Pyramidal Cells:
        Most common neuron type in the cerebral cortex and hippocampus.
        Distinct pyramid-shaped cell body.
        Extensive dendritic trees (apical and basal) for integrating inputs.
        Typically projection neurons with long axons connecting brain regions.
        Primary computational units and output neurons of the cortex.
    - Spiny Stellate Cells:
        Found mainly in Layer 4 of the cortex.
        Star-shaped dendritic tree, covered in spines.
        Receive major input from the thalamus.
        Primarily local circuit excitatory neurons, though some can project short distances.

B. Inhibitory Neurons (Interneurons)
    Title: The "Stop" Signals / Local Circuit Modulators
    Primary Neurotransmitter: GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) or Glycine (in spinal cord/brainstem)
    Primary Effect: Inhibitory (decrease the likelihood of postsynaptic firing)
    Typical Firing Patterns: Diverse! Includes:
    Fast-Spiking (FS): High frequency, little adaptation (e.g., PV+ neurons).
    Non-Fast-Spiking types: Various patterns including adapting, bursting, irregular (e.g., SOM+, VIP+ neurons).
    General Role: Primarily local circuit modulation, shaping activity, controlling timing, preventing runaway excitation, generating oscillations.
    Common Examples (Diversity is High):
    - Parvalbumin-expressing (PV+) Interneurons:
        Often Fast-Spiking (FS).
        Includes Basket Cells (target soma/proximal dendrites of pyramidal cells) and Chandelier Cells (target axon initial segment).
        Provide powerful, precisely timed inhibition; crucial for controlling output and generating gamma oscillations.
        Typically local circuit neurons.
        Can inhibit the neuron that was used to fire itself.
    - Somatostatin-expressing (SOM+) Interneurons:
        Includes Martinotti Cells (target distal dendrites of pyramidal cells).
        Often show adapting firing patterns.
        Involved in feedback inhibition, filtering dendritic inputs.
        Typically local circuit neurons.
        Can inhibit the neuron that was used to fire itself.
    - Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide-expressing (VIP+) Interneurons:
        Often target other interneurons (especially SOM+ cells), leading to disinhibition of pyramidal cells.
        Play roles in cortical state changes, learning, attention.
        Typically local circuit neurons.
